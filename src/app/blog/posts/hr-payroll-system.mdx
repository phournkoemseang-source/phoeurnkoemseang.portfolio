## Overview

An HR & Payroll Management System is one of those projects that looks simple on the surface and gets complicated fast. Employees, salaries, attendance, payroll runs — every piece depends on the others, and one wrong calculation ripples through the whole company.

This project tackled all of it with a strong focus on **OOP (Object-Oriented Programming)** principles, keeping the growing system modular, testable, and maintainable.

## The Problem

HR systems deal in sensitive, precise data. The core requirements were:

- Manage **employee records** reliably
- Calculate **salaries** based on role, hours, and attendance
- Track **attendance** day to day
- Process **payroll** correctly and repeatably

With a small team and a tight timeframe, we needed a structure that wouldn't collapse as features piled up — hence the emphasis on OOP.

## The Tech Stack

The backend runs on **Node.js** with **TypeScript** for type safety, and **MySQL** handles the database. **Postman** was used to test APIs and endpoints throughout development.

### Architecture Notes

Designing the domain with OOP meant modeling real-world concepts directly: an `Employee` owns an attendance history, a `PayrollRun` aggregates salaries, and calculations live where they belong rather than scattered across route handlers. That separation made the trickiest parts — salary math and payroll processing — much easier to reason about and test.

## Key Contributions

- Built the HR & Payroll logic with strong **OOP** principles
- **MySQL** database management for employee and payroll data
- **Postman** for testing APIs and endpoints
- Collaborated using **GitHub** and AI tools
- Assisted in **UX/UI design**
- Deployed the application on **Render**

## What I Learned

1. **OOP is about taming complexity.** When entities and their behaviors map cleanly to classes, features like payroll stop being scary.
2. **Precision work needs type safety.** TypeScript caught many mistakes before they reached the database — a huge win for money-related logic.
3. **Collaboration tools matter.** GitHub, AI tooling, and clear UX review helped a small team ship a big system.
